# DEVELOPMENT BRIEF: “SMALL TALK” - Daily Opening Questions

## New Feature for JHS English Teaching Games Application

-----

## 🎯 FEATURE OVERVIEW

**Feature Name:** Small Talk (スモールトーク)

**Category:** Daily Warm-up Activities

**Purpose:** Provide teachers with ready-made daily opening questions that align with students’ current grammar level and textbook progression, solving three critical pain points:

1. Teachers don’t have to create new questions every day
1. Structured classroom activity management with built-in timing
1. Scaffolding to help students produce full, grammatically correct sentences

**Target Users:**

- Japanese junior high school English teachers (ALTs and JTEs)
- All three grade levels (中1, 中2, 中3)
- Used daily at the start of every English class (5-10 minutes)

-----

## 📚 PEDAGOGICAL FOUNDATION

### Core Concept

“Small Talk” provides **daily conversation starters** that are:

- ✅ **Grammar-aligned**: Questions only use grammar points students have learned
- ✅ **Cumulative**: Questions recycle previous grammar while introducing current points
- ✅ **Scaffolded**: Built-in support helps students produce complete sentences
- ✅ **Flexible**: Teachers can adapt to their actual pacing and schedule
- ✅ **Fun**: Mix of real and imaginative scenarios keeps students engaged

### Three Sub-Games Under “Small Talk” Category

#### 1️⃣ **TALK ABOUT YOU** (Grade 1 - 中1)

- **Grammar Focus:** Be verbs, Can, Simple Present, Present Progressive, Have/Has
- **Question Types:** Personal experiences, daily routines, abilities, preferences
- **Example:** “What time do you usually wake up? Why do you wake up at that time?”
- **Sentence Pattern:** “I usually wake up at [time] because…”

#### 2️⃣ **WHAT IF?** (Grade 2 - 中2)

- **Grammar Focus:** Past Simple, Future (will/going to), Real Conditionals, Comparatives, Infinitives, Gerunds, Passive Voice
- **Question Types:** Past experiences, future plans, real-world choices, comparisons
- **Example:** “If you have free time this weekend, what will you do? Why?”
- **Sentence Pattern:** “If I have free time, I will… because…”

#### 3️⃣ **IMAGINE** (Grade 3 - 中3)

- **Grammar Focus:** Present Perfect, Second Conditional, Relative Clauses, Indirect Questions, Third Conditional (advanced)
- **Question Types:** Hypothetical situations, past reflections, complex scenarios
- **Example:** “If you were super talented in a sport, what player would you be? Why would you be that player?”
- **Sentence Pattern:** “If I were talented in sports, I would be… because…”

## 🗓️ CONTENT STRUCTURE & ORGANIZATION

### Question Bank Size

- **40 weeks** of school year (April - March in Japanese calendar)
- **5 school days** per week
- **200 questions per grade** (40 weeks × 5 days)
- **600 total questions** across all three grades

### Grammar Progression Logic

#### **CRITICAL RULE: Cumulative Grammar Use**

Questions can ONLY use grammar points that students:

1. Have already learned in previous grades
1. Have learned so far in the current year

**Example for Grade 2, Week 20 (October):**

- ✅ Can use: Be verbs, Can, Simple Present, Present Progressive (Grade 1)
- ✅ Can use: Past Simple, Future, Real Conditionals, Infinitives (Grade 2, learned by Week 20)
- ❌ Cannot use: Comparatives (not learned until Week 25)
- ❌ Cannot use: Second Conditional (Grade 3 grammar)

### Weekly Organization Structure

```
Small Talk
├── Grade 1 (Talk About You)
│   ├── Week 1-4 (April): Be verbs - introductions, descriptions
│   ├── Week 5-8 (May): Can/Cannot - abilities, permissions
│   ├── Week 9-12 (June): Simple Present - routines, habits
│   ├── Week 13-16 (July): Present Progressive - current actions
│   ├── Week 17-20 (September): Questions (Do you...? / Does he...?)
│   ├── Week 21-24 (October): Review & mixed grammar
│   ├── Week 25-28 (November): How many/much, Whose
│   ├── Week 29-32 (December): Have/Has, possessives
│   ├── Week 33-36 (January-February): Past Simple introduction
│   └── Week 37-40 (March): Year review
│
├── Grade 2 (What If?)
│   ├── Week 1-4 (April): Past Simple - spring vacation, memories
│   ├── Week 5-8 (May): Future (going to/will) - plans, predictions
│   ├── Week 9-12 (June): Real Conditionals (If...will) - choices
│   ├── Week 13-16 (July): Infinitives (to + verb) - purposes, feelings
│   ├── Week 17-20 (September): Gerunds (verb + ing) - hobbies
│   ├── Week 21-24 (October): Comparatives & Superlatives - preferences
│   ├── Week 25-28 (November): Indirect Questions - polite inquiry
│   ├── Week 29-32 (December): Passive Voice - descriptions
│   ├── Week 33-36 (January-February): Must/Have to - obligations
│   └── Week 37-40 (March): Year review
│
└── Grade 3 (Imagine)
    ├── Week 1-4 (April): Present Perfect - experiences
    ├── Week 5-8 (May): Present Perfect Continuous - ongoing activities
    ├── Week 9-12 (June): Infinitives (for... to...) - purposes
    ├── Week 13-16 (July): Causative verbs (make/let/help)
    ├── Week 17-20 (September): Indirect Questions - embedded questions
    ├── Week 21-24 (October): Relative Clauses (who/which/that) - descriptions
    ├── Week 25-28 (November): Second Conditional (If I were...) - hypotheticals
    ├── Week 29-32 (December): Reduced Relative Clauses - advanced descriptions
    ├── Week 33-36 (January-February): Wish clauses - regrets and desires
    └── Week 37-40 (March): Third Conditional - past hypotheticals (advanced)
```

## 🎮 TEACHER CUSTOMIZATION FEATURES

### Essential Customization Options

1. **Edit Existing Questions**
- Click “Edit Question” button
- Modify English or Japanese text
- Save as custom version (preserves original)
- Revert to original anytime
1. **Add Custom Questions**
- “Add New Question” button
- Form with all fields (question, grammar point, example, etc.)
- Assign to specific week/day
- Automatically integrates into sequence
1. **Skip Questions**
- “Skip This Question” button
- Removed from rotation but not deleted
- Can be un-skipped later
- Useful if topic is culturally inappropriate or recently used
1. **Reorder Questions**
- Drag-and-drop interface
- Move questions between days/weeks
- Useful for adapting to actual class pace
- “Reset to Default Order” option
1. **Favorites System**
- Star/heart icon to favorite questions
- “My Favorites” collection
- Quick access to most-used questions
- Can create themed collections
1. **Question History**
- Track which questions have been used
- Mark as “Used on [date]”
- Filter to show unused questions
- Prevent repetition across years

## 🎯 SUCCESS METRICS & TEACHER FEEDBACK

### How Teachers Will Use This Feature

**Typical Classroom Flow (8-10 minutes total):**

1. **Display Question** (30 seconds)
- Teacher opens Small Talk app
- Projects question on screen
- Reads question aloud in English and Japanese
1. **Think Time** (1-2 minutes)
- Students think individually
- Can write notes in notebooks
- Teacher starts timer on screen
1. **Pair Talk** (2-3 minutes)
- Students discuss with partner
- Practice saying full sentences
- Teacher monitors and assists
- Timer visible on screen
1. **Share Time** (3-5 minutes)
- 3-5 volunteers share answers with class
- Teacher uses follow-up questions to extend
- Teacher corrects major grammar errors gently
- Example answer revealed if class struggles
1. **Transition** (30 seconds)
- Quick recap of grammar pattern
- Connect to main lesson topic
- Close Small Talk, begin main class activity

### Expected Outcomes

- **Daily speaking practice** for every student
- **Grammar recycling** in natural contexts
- **Student confidence** increases with routine
- **Teacher preparation time** reduced significantly
- **Consistent quality** across all classes
- **Cultural knowledge** building (through varied topics)

### Teacher Feedback Integration

Build in feedback mechanisms:

- “Was this question appropriate?” (Yes/No/Edit)
- “How did students respond?” (Great/OK/Struggled)
- “Report an issue” button
- Star rating system
- Comment field for improvement suggestions

## 📋 CONTENT CREATION REQUIREMENTS

### Question Writing Guidelines

All questions must meet these criteria:

✅ **Grammatically Accurate**

- Use only grammar students have learned
- Clear, unambiguous sentence structure
- Natural, spoken English (not overly formal)

✅ **Age-Appropriate**

- Relevant to 12-15 year old Japanese students
- Culturally sensitive to Japanese context
- Avoid controversial topics (politics, religion, etc.)
- Consider family diversity (single parents, international families, etc.)

✅ **Engaging & Varied**

- Mix of personal, imaginative, and opinion questions
- Balance serious and fun topics
- Avoid repetitive question structures
- Include both individual and universal topics

✅ **Scaffolded for Success**

- Sentence starters provided
- Example answer demonstrates target length (2-4 sentences)
- Key vocabulary supplied when needed
- Follow-up questions help extend weak answers

✅ **Inclusive & Positive**

- No questions that could embarrass students
- Respect different abilities and backgrounds
- Allow for multiple “correct” answers
- Encourage personal expression

### Topics to Include

**Universal Topics (All Grades):**

- Food, sports, animals, colors, seasons
- Family, friends, school, hobbies
- Music, movies, anime, games
- Travel, nature, technology

**Grade-Specific Topics:**

**Grade 1:** Concrete, present-focused

- Daily routines, school life
- Abilities and talents
- Simple preferences
- Immediate plans

**Grade 2:** Past/future, comparisons

- Memories and experiences
- Future goals and plans
- Opinions and preferences
- Problem-solving scenarios

**Grade 3:** Abstract, reflective

- Life experiences and lessons
- Hypothetical situations
- Complex opinions
- Future aspirations
- Global issues (age-appropriate)

### Topics to Avoid or Handle Carefully

❌ **Completely Avoid:**

- Questions about physical appearance or weight
- Questions about family income or possessions
- Questions about romantic relationships
- Questions about grades or academic ability
- Questions that could reveal bullying situations

⚠️ **Handle with Care:**

- Family structure (allow for diverse families)
- Money and shopping (keep amounts reasonable)
- Cultural practices (respect different traditions)
- Health and food (consider dietary restrictions)
- Future careers (don’t pressure about decisions)

## 💡 DESIGN INSPIRATION & VISUAL STYLE

### Design Principles

**For Classroom Projection:**

- **Large, clear typography** (minimum 36pt for main question)
- **High contrast** (dark text on light background or vice versa)
- **Minimal distractions** (clean, uncluttered layout)
- **Touch-friendly** buttons (minimum 44×44px tap targets)
- **Intuitive icons** (universally understood symbols)

Consider using consistent colors for each grade:

- **Grade 1:** Blue tones (beginner, calm, foundational)
- **Grade 2:** Green tones (growing, progressing)
- **Grade 3:** Purple/violet tones (advanced, sophisticated)

Or use colors for grammar categories:

- **Past tenses:** Orange/amber (looking back)
- **Present tenses:** Blue/green (current state)
- **Future tenses:** Purple/violet (forward-looking)
- **Conditionals:** Pink/magenta (hypothetical/imaginative)

- **Main Question:** Bold, sans-serif, 40-48pt
- **Japanese Translation:** Regular, sans-serif, 24-28pt
- **Grammar Pattern:** Monospace or distinct font, 20-24pt
- **Body Text:** Sans-serif, 16-20pt
- **UI Labels:** Sans-serif, 14-16pt

## 🎓 PEDAGOGICAL NOTES FOR DEVELOPMENT TEAM

### Understanding the Japanese Context

**Why This Feature Matters:**

1. **Class Size:** Japanese JHS classes average 35-40 students, making individual speaking practice challenging. This warm-up ensures every student gets at least 2-3 minutes of speaking time daily.
1. **Grammar-Translation Method:** Japanese English education historically emphasizes grammar rules and translation. This feature bridges that approach with communicative practice.
1. **Teacher Preparation Time:** Japanese teachers have heavy workloads. Pre-made questions save 15-30 minutes of daily prep time while maintaining quality.
1. **Consistency Across Classes:** With 5 classes per grade, same-day questions ensure fair and equal education for all students.
1. **ALT-JTE Collaboration:** This tool helps both Assistant Language Teachers (ALTs) and Japanese Teachers of English (JTEs) conduct effective warm-ups without extensive planning.

### Key Educational Principles

**Scaffolding:**

- Questions build from simple to complex
- Grammar cumulates (never leaves behind previous learning)
- Support materials (sentence starters, examples) fade as students progress

**Student-Centered:**

- Personal questions allow individual expression
- No single “correct” answer (except grammar accuracy)
- Students can share as much or as little as comfortable

**Formative Assessment:**

- Teacher hears all students speak weekly
- Can identify students needing extra support
- Tracks grammar accuracy informally
- Not graded, low-pressure practice

**Cultural Responsiveness:**

- Questions appropriate for Japanese cultural context
- Avoid topics that could cause discomfort
- Include both universal and Japan-specific content
- Respect diverse family structures and backgrounds
